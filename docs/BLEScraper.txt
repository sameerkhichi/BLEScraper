Bluetooth

The download uses CoreBluetooth and then disconnects. The iOS build discovers two services and four characteristics: UART write 6E400002 and notify 6E400003 on 6E40FFF0-B5A3-F393-E0A9-E50E24DCCA9E, and big-data notify DE5BF729 and write DE5BF72A on DE5BF728-D711-4E47-AF26-65E3012A5DC7. It does not discover any other characteristic or descriptor. Turning notifications on writes the client-config descriptor of those two notify characteristics. iPhone shows a Pair sheet only when a read or write comes back Insufficient Authentication. A build that only limited the service list still walked every characteristic and every descriptor of those two services, so the card can show two services while the sheet still appears. Tapping Pair stores a bond. Gesture keys can stay quiet right after that and start the next morning, when iOS connects the saved bond. This app does not open HID for that. Forget the ring again after this build is installed, and do not tap Pair. If a notify still returns Insufficient Authentication, the app drops the link. A phone that is powered off cannot download, because Bluetooth is off.

The Ring card lists the service UUIDs from the last connection, under Disconnect ring. That list is saved on the phone and is not sent to Solloid. After a normal download it is the two services above.

An R09 normally exposes more than the app opens:

- 6E40FFF0-B5A3-F393-E0A9-E50E24DCCA9E is the Nordic UART service. The app writes 6E400002 and listens on 6E400003. Heart rate, steps, battery, live sensors, and the wave detector go through here.
- DE5BF728-D711-4E47-AF26-65E3012A5DC7 is the vendor big-data service. Sleep and the larger stored logs go through here.
- 00001812-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB is Human Interface Device, the media remote. The app does not open this.
- 00001800-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Generic Access, 00001801-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Generic Attribute, 0000180A-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Device Information, and 0000180F-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Battery are the other standard services these rings commonly advertise. Battery in the app is read with the UART command, not the standard battery service.

The card is the list for this ring. The usual set above is what these rings advertise, not a promise that this connection opened each one.

Automatic sync

The phone does not have to stay open. About every 4 hours iOS or Android wakes the app, it connects, downloads the stored record, sends it, and disconnects. Keep syncing arms the first wake. After a wake finishes, the next one is scheduled without opening the app. The download does not wait for Wi-Fi. A notification is sent when the wake starts and when it finishes, and the home screen keeps the last one. iOS picks the real time and will not wake the app after it is swiped away. Android shows a notification while that download is running.

That wake reads about 7 days of stored heart rate, steps, stress, and HRV, plus the sleep nights and the stored blood-oxygen and temperature slots still on the ring. It still runs if the ring is on the table. One successful wake can recover a missed day. The phone does not start a live heart-rate, oxygen, or temperature session on that wake.

The ring writes heart rate on the interval saved in its own settings: 5, 15, 30, or 60 minutes. The stored heart-rate log still has a slot every 5 minutes, so a longer interval leaves some slots empty. Blood oxygen, stress, and HRV follow the cadences the firmware chooses. SpO2 and temperature come back as half-hour slots in vitalSpots. The phone keeps every spot it has saved. A later download updates a spot with the same timestamp and leaves older spots in place. Solloid should keep each spot by its timestamp. A later snapshot that no longer includes an old spot does not mean that spot should be deleted. A phone that is powered off cannot download them, because Bluetooth is off. A download does not change these settings.

Confirmed through observation the ring does check heart rate every 5 minutes flat XX:05, XX:10 and so on. The red light for blood oxygen level does turn on hourly at XX:32. These times could change but they do indeed trigger. HRV, Step, calories, stress, distance all are also read on the ring confirmed through packets.

Leaving the app open does not start extra sensor checks. Opening the app runs a download when one is due. The Measure buttons still run only when tapped.

Sleep on the phone

A wake stores the sleep nights still on the ring, whether or not the ring is being worn. Sleep is part of the same download as heart rate and steps, about every 4 hours. The ring can be nearby but off a finger. That read does not turn the lights on, and it does not replace the heart-rate or step samples already saved. The nights are merged into the saved reading by startedAt. A night the ring sends again replaces that start time. A night the ring no longer has stays on the phone. The Sleep screen and the upload queue both keep that merged list. Sync ring does the same thing if the ring is not on a finger. If the ring does not answer, the next check tries again. A failed sleep read does not wait out the full 4 hours.

Sleep score

The ring does not report a score. The phone estimates one from the stages already stored and labels it estimated. Latency is left out. startedAt is when the ring opened the session, not when the person got into bed, so the phone does not pretend to know how long it took to fall asleep. The other four weights are divided by 0.95 so they still sum to 1:

score = (0.30 duration + 0.25 efficiency + 0.25 architecture + 0.15 continuity) / 0.95

The result is clamped to 0–100 and rounded. 85 or more is Excellent. 70 or more is Good. 50 or more is Fair. Anything lower is Poor.

Duration uses minutesAsleep, which is light + deep + rem. 7 through 9 hours scores 100. The short side is 0 at 0 hours, 19 at 4, 49 at 5, 79 at 6, and 100 at 7. The long side mirrors that: 79 at 10 hours, 49 at 11, 19 at 12, and 0 at 13 or more. Values between those hours are linear.

Efficiency is minutesAsleep divided by minutesInBed, the ring's session window. 90% or more scores 100. 60% or less scores 0. Between those is linear. A session with no in-bed time scores 0.

Architecture is the share of minutesAsleep in each stage. Deep scores 100 from 13% to 23%. REM scores 100 from 20% to 25%. Light scores 100 from 50% to 60%. Each percentage point outside a band costs 5 points, and a stage score cannot go below 0. Architecture is 45% deep, 45% REM, and 10% light. Awake time is not in this part, so it is not counted twice. No time asleep scores 0.

Continuity uses minutesAwake and awakenings. 20 awake minutes or fewer scores 100, and 60 or more scores 0. Fewer than 2 awakenings scores 100, and 6 or more scores 0. Between each pair is linear. Continuity is the average of those two. The longest awake stage is shown when a night is expanded. It is not part of the score.

If the last stage that is not trailing awake time is deep, the phone subtracts 5 points after the weighted sum and the card says "Ended in deep sleep."

Sleep debt uses 8 hours as the need. Nights are walked from oldest startedAt to newest, including nights the ring has already dropped and the phone kept. Each night adds minutesAsleep minus 480. Extra sleep pays the balance down, and the balance never goes above 0, so a long night does not create a credit. sleepDebtMinutes on a night is the minutes still owed after that night. The home sleep card and the newest history card show that balance. 0 is "No sleep debt."

Raw and Both stamp score, scoreLabel, endedInDeepSleep, and sleepDebtMinutes onto each night at send time, including a body that was already waiting in the queue. A live reading that sends "sleep": [] stays an empty list. Solloid can read the newest night's sleepDebtMinutes as the balance on the card. Processed puts those same fields on the nights inside processed.sleep and leaves the sample arrays out. Debt is still walked across every stored night, so a night inside a Today or This week window keeps the balance the card shows.

Stress score

The ring stores its own stress estimate, from 1 to 99, about every 30 minutes. Command 0x37 downloads that history. Command 0x36 turns the logging on and off with the other automatic checks. Measure stress asks for one live reading with command 0x69, sensor type 0x08, and the phone saves that sample with the time it was taken. Zero and anything over 99 are ignored. The raw samples are the stress array in the JSON. The averages, bands, and trend are not stored as their own fields. Processed and Both add them under processed.stress when the reading is posted.

These bands are labels in the app. They are not a confirmed QRing scale, and a 50 here is not a 50 on another device. 0–24 is Relaxed. 25–49 is Low. 50–74 is Moderate. 75–99 is High.

A sample is daytime when it falls outside every sleep window, from startedAt up to but not including endedAt. The Today circle shows the newest saved stress sample, including an overnight sample or one from an earlier day, with that band under it. The line under the circles is today's daytime average and peak. With no daytime sample it says "No daytime stress yet."

The Stress card, above Sleep, shows that same newest sample, and the Taken time is the sample's timestamp. A newer Measure still wins until a later stored sample arrives. The graph draws the stored stress series in time order, including samples that are not daytime. With no stored sample it says "No stress samples yet." The average, the peak, how long readings stayed at 75 or above, the share of samples in each band, and whether the last 3 hours are Rising, Falling, or Steady against the earlier part of today stay on today's daytime samples. That trend needs at least two samples on each side, and a gap of 5 points. High time counts each high sample as the gap until the next sample, capped at 60 minutes. The last sample of the day counts as 30 minutes.

A 7-day average of earlier daytime samples appears once two earlier days exist. It says how many days it used when that is under 7. A 30-day average appears once 8 earlier days exist. The ring keeps about a week, so the longer average only fills in as the phone keeps older samples.

Overnight is the average of samples inside the latest sleep window. It is labeled Overnight and is not given a stress band. A higher overnight reading is kept apart from the daytime number.

Stress history opens from the Stress card. It shows one month at a time, Sunday through Saturday. Each day with daytime samples shows that day's average, colored by the same bands, with the peak and the minimum under it. Previous and next month, and previous and next year, move the grid. Next month and next year stop at the current month. A day opens the samples the phone saved for that local day, drawn from midnight to midnight. Days with no daytime sample stay empty. The ring still keeps about a week, so a day only appears after the phone has stored it. The history reads the saved stress array and does not download older logs or change the upload.

Heart rate history, blood oxygen history, and body temperature history each open from their own card. They use that same month grid. A day with samples shows the average, then the peak and the minimum. Heart rate and blood oxygen round the average to a whole number. Temperature rounds it to one decimal. The average is colored red for heart rate, by the blood-oxygen bands for SpO2, and by the temperature stage for body temperature. A day lists every saved reading with its local time. Overnight samples stay in that local day. Empty heart-rate slots stay out. A blood-oxygen day ignores a spot that has only temperature, and a temperature day ignores a spot that has only blood oxygen. Days the phone never stored stay empty. These screens read the saved heartRate array and vitalSpots. They do not download older logs or change the upload. The ring still keeps about a week. The phone keeps the heart-rate samples and vital spots it has already stored.

Activity history opens from the Today card. The month grid shows that day's total steps, with the distance and calories under them. A day with a stored block appears, including a day whose totals are zero. A day with no blocks stays empty. Opening a day lists each 15-minute block with its local time, steps, distance, and calories, and the day's three totals sit above that list. It reads the saved steps array. It does not download older logs or change the upload.

Body temperature

The card shows the newest saved finger reading in °C, with Fahrenheit and the stage beside it. That reading is the latest vitalSpots entry with a temperature, from a download or from Measure, and the Taken time is that spot's timestamp. A newer Measure still wins until a later stored spot arrives. The bodyTemperatureC field on the snapshot is only the newest spot from the last download, so the card does not read that field. The phone notification still uses bodyTemperatureC. The thermometer runs from 24°C to 40°C.

38°C (100.4°F) or higher is Fever. 35.0°C up to 38°C is Normal. Below 35°C (95°F) is Mild hypothermia, and that band runs down to 32°C. Below 32°C (89.6°F) is Moderate hypothermia. Below 28°C (82.4°F) is Severe hypothermia. Readings under 28°C used to be discarded. They are kept now so a severe reading can alert. A value still warming up, or anything under 20°C or over 43°C, is still ignored.

A Measure tap or a background sync notifies from that latest reading. The first time it crosses a line, the phone notifies. A worse line notifies again right away. The same stage waits 4 hours, then reminds. A normal reading clears the notice. Opening the app uses the saved reading the same way. A sync that does not include a temperature leaves the last notice alone.

The titles are "Body temperature is high", "Body temperature is low", "Body temperature is very low", and "Body temperature is dangerously low". Android posts these on a Body temperature channel. The stage is not its own JSON field. Raw and Both still send bodyTemperatureC. Processed sends the day's average, peak, and minimum under processed.temperature.

Ring storage

The R09 does not keep a month. The useful store is about a week: heart rate in 5-minute slots (288 a day) once automatic checks are on, steps in 15-minute blocks (96 a day), stored blood oxygen and temperature in half-hour slots, and roughly a week of sleep nights. Days older than that come back empty from the ring. A full week of JSON is a few hundred kilobytes, not a large flash log. There is no published chip size worth planning around.

What the phone keeps

The ring still only has about a week to send. Once a sample is on the phone, a later download does not delete it. The same timestamp is updated with the value the ring just sent. A missed week cannot be filled in later, because the ring no longer has those days.

Steps, calories, and distance are one 15-minute block. They stay, with no age cutoff. Heart rate stays the same way. Stress stays the same way. Blood oxygen and body temperature stay in vitalSpots, with no 10-day cutoff. A spot that has only one of those fields does not erase the other field already saved at that timestamp. Sleep stays too. A night is matched by startedAt. The ring sending that night again replaces it. A night the ring no longer has stays on the phone.

Duplicates

The phone merges heart rate, steps, stress, and vital spots by timestamp, and sleep by startedAt, so the saved reading does not grow a second copy of the same minute or the same night. Raw and Both still post that whole snapshot whenever any sample is new, so the same minute can be posted again inside the next body. Processed posts the summary for the selected window instead of the sample arrays. That snapshot can now be older than the week still on the ring.

A month-long cache is not worth it. The ring cannot resend anything older than about a week, so a month of keys does not make the filter safer. If Solloid appends rows, send only timestamps that were not in the last successful upload, and remember those timestamps for about 10 days. If Solloid replaces a row by timestamp, the full snapshot is fine and the phone does not need a second cache.

Two-day last reading

The Last reading Steps number is two days because, while the app is open, every log read asks the ring for two days. It is not a pile of readings that have not been sent yet.

While the phone is disconnected, the ring keeps its own history. Steps are stored in 15-minute blocks. The heart-rate log has a slot every 5 minutes. The ring fills those slots on the interval saved in its settings, which can be 5, 15, 30, or 60 minutes. The ring does not send those blocks on its own. They stay on the ring until the phone connects and asks. A download reads them and does not change the saved interval.

On a download, about every 4 hours or when Sync ring is tapped, the phone connects, sets the ring clock to UTC, and requests the stored logs. Steps are yesterday (day offset 1) and today (day offset 0). Heart rate is the log for those same two days. The ring replies with the blocks it has stored. The phone saves that reply as the last reading and disconnects. Days older than yesterday can still sit on the ring. This open-app read does not ask for them.

Last reading Steps adds every step block in that reply, so the number is yesterday plus today. The Today card does not. It keeps the blocks that fall on the phone's local calendar day. A successful upload does not drop yesterday from the next read. The next open-app sync asks for two days again.

A background wake, described above, asks for about 7 days instead. The phone also keeps earlier steps, heart rate, stress, blood oxygen, body temperature, and sleep, so after a wake the saved reading can cover more than the two days an open-app sync just requested. The ring still returns nothing older than about a week.

Sending to the Arduino IP

The last reading and the upload queue are different. After a read, the phone compares the new payload with the previous one. If something changed, the whole payload is added to a queue stored on the phone. The queue keeps that full reading. About once an hour, and when Send waiting readings is tapped, the app POSTs each queued body as JSON to the API URL in Settings. Sync ring sends that queue right away. That URL is the Arduino, reached through the home network and port forwarding, for example http://203.0.113.10:8080/ingest. An optional key is sent as the X-Api-Key header. A blank URL is not posted. A URL has to start with http:// or https://. An address with no scheme is rejected, and a response outside 200 through 299 leaves that reading in the queue. If the phone cannot reach that address, the queued readings stay on the phone and are tried again later. Sending or keeping that queue does not change what the ring returns on the next read.

The Arduino card on Config chooses what that POST contains. The default is Raw. Raw is the sample JSON in Sleep upload, with no processed field. Processed sends source, syncedAt, ring, and processed, and leaves the sample arrays out. Both sends the Raw JSON and adds processed. The choice is saved on the phone and applied when the reading is sent, including a reading that was already waiting. Today, This week, and All limit processed only. Today is the phone's local calendar day. This week is the seven local days ending today. All uses every day stored on the phone. Raw sample arrays are not trimmed.

processed.heartRate, processed.spo2, and processed.temperature are one row per local day in the window, with key, average, peak, and minimum. key is YYYY-MM-DD on the phone, not the UTC date. processed.activity is one row per local day with key, steps, calories, and distanceMeters. Those rows do not repeat the samples. processed.stress carries the card's today fields: current, average, peak, highMinutes, distribution, trend, overnight, week, and month. Its days array is the daytime average, peak, and minimum for each local day in the window. processed.sleep.nights lists nights that overlap the window, each with startedAt, endedAt, score, scoreLabel, endedInDeepSleep, and sleepDebtMinutes. processed.sleep.debtMinutes is the newest of those nights. processed.window is day, week, or all.

Config, at the bottom of the screen, can delete history records already saved on the phone. The user picks a start and an end in local time. Heart rate, steps, stress, HRV, blood oxygen, temperature, and sleep inside that range are removed from the saved reading. A sleep night is one record, so a range that covers any part of the night removes that night. Packets waiting to send stay in the queue and can still be uploaded. Nothing on the ring is erased, and a live read still pulls whatever the ring has. The ring keeps its own logs, so a later sync that reads those days can save them on the phone again.

Sleep upload

Sync posts one JSON body to the API URL (HTTP or the port-forwarded Arduino IP). Content-Type is application/json. Raw and Both use the shape below. A live sensor reading uses that same shape and sends "sleep": []. Processed replaces this body with source, syncedAt, ring, and processed.

Timestamps are UTC ISO-8601. The app sets the ring clock to UTC, and sleep start/end are minutes after that midnight. If start is later than end, sleep began the evening before the wake-up day.

The ring sends stages, not a score and not cycle numbers. Raw and Both add the estimate from Sleep score when they post. Stage names are light, deep, rem, awake, and unknown (any code other than 2, 3, 4, or 5). minutesAsleep is light + deep + rem. minutesInBed is the start-to-end window. awakenings is how many awake stages were stored. A cycle is the phone grouping stages through the next REM block. complete is false when that stretch ended without REM. The JSON below is a Raw body.
