Bluetooth

The download uses CoreBluetooth and then disconnects. The iOS build discovers two services and four characteristics: UART write 6E400002 and notify 6E400003 on 6E40FFF0-B5A3-F393-E0A9-E50E24DCCA9E, and big-data notify DE5BF729 and write DE5BF72A on DE5BF728-D711-4E47-AF26-65E3012A5DC7. It does not discover any other characteristic or descriptor. Turning notifications on writes the client-config descriptor of those two notify characteristics. iPhone shows a Pair sheet only when a read or write comes back Insufficient Authentication. A build that only limited the service list still walked every characteristic and every descriptor of those two services, so the card can show two services while the sheet still appears. Tapping Pair stores a bond. Gesture keys can stay quiet right after that and start the next morning, when iOS connects the saved bond. This app does not open HID for that. Forget the ring again after this build is installed, and do not tap Pair. If a notify still returns Insufficient Authentication, the app drops the link. A phone that is powered off cannot download, because Bluetooth is off.

The Ring card lists the service UUIDs from the last connection, under Disconnect ring. That list is saved on the phone and is not sent to Solloid. After a normal download it is the two services above.

An R09 normally exposes more than the app opens:

- 6E40FFF0-B5A3-F393-E0A9-E50E24DCCA9E is the Nordic UART service. The app writes 6E400002 and listens on 6E400003. Heart rate, steps, battery, live sensors, and the wave detector go through here.
- DE5BF728-D711-4E47-AF26-65E3012A5DC7 is the vendor big-data service. Sleep and the larger stored logs go through here.
- 00001812-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB is Human Interface Device, the media remote. The app does not open this.
- 00001800-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Generic Access, 00001801-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Generic Attribute, 0000180A-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Device Information, and 0000180F-0000-1000-8000-00805F9B34FB Battery are the other standard services these rings commonly advertise. Battery in the app is read with the UART command, not the standard battery service.

The card is the list for this ring. The usual set above is what these rings advertise, not a promise that this connection opened each one.

Automatic sync

The phone does not have to stay open. About every 4 hours iOS or Android wakes the app, it connects, downloads the stored record, sends it, and disconnects. Keep syncing arms the first wake. After a wake finishes, the next one is scheduled without opening the app. The download does not wait for Wi-Fi. A notification is sent when the wake starts and when it finishes, and the home screen keeps the last one. iOS picks the real time and will not wake the app after it is swiped away. Android shows a notification while that download is running.

That wake reads about 7 days of stored heart rate, steps, stress, and HRV, plus the sleep nights and the stored blood-oxygen and temperature slots still on the ring. It still runs if the ring is on the table. One successful wake can recover a missed day. The phone does not start a live heart-rate, oxygen, or temperature session on that wake.

The ring writes heart rate every 5 minutes once its own check is on. Blood oxygen, stress, and HRV follow that cycle. SpO2 and temperature come back as half-hour slots in vitalSpots. The phone keeps about 10 days of these spots. Solloid should keep each spot by its timestamp. A later snapshot that no longer includes an old spot does not mean that spot should be deleted. A phone that is powered off cannot download them, because Bluetooth is off.

Leaving the app open does not start extra sensor checks. Opening the app runs a download when one is due. The Measure buttons still run only when tapped.

Sleep on the phone

A wake stores the sleep nights still on the ring, whether or not the ring is being worn. Sleep is part of the same download as heart rate and steps, about every 4 hours. The ring can be nearby but off a finger. That read does not turn the lights on, and it does not replace the heart-rate or step samples already saved. The nights are written into the saved reading, so the Sleep screen and the upload queue both keep them. Sync ring does the same thing if the ring is not on a finger. If the ring does not answer, the next check tries again. A failed sleep read does not wait out the full 4 hours.

Sleep score

The ring does not report a score. The phone estimates one for the Sleep screen and labels it estimated. That number is not stored as its own field and is not sent in the JSON. Solloid can recompute it from the stages.

minutesAsleep is light + deep + rem. Awake and unknown minutes are left out of that total. awakenings is how many awake stages were stored. minutesAwake is the sum of those awake stages.

The score is three parts, then clamped to 0–100 and rounded.

Duration. Eight hours asleep scores 100. Each hour shorter or longer than 8 costs 15 points, and the result cannot go below 0. Six hours and ten hours both score 70. Under about 1 hour 20 minutes, or over about 14 hours 40 minutes, duration scores 0.

Restorative sleep. This is the percent of minutesAsleep that was deep or REM. Light sleep is in the total, so it lowers this percent. A night with no time asleep scores 0. Unknown minutes are not part of this percent.

Awake penalty. Each awakening costs 8 points, and each minute awake costs one third of a point. The penalty stops at 40.

The score is half the duration part plus half the restorative part, minus the awake penalty. 85 or more is Excellent. 70 or more is Good. 50 or more is Fair. Anything lower is Poor.

Stress score

The ring stores its own stress estimate, from 1 to 99, about every 30 minutes. Command 0x37 downloads that history. Command 0x36 turns the logging on and off with the other automatic checks. Measure stress asks for one live reading with command 0x69, sensor type 0x08, and the phone saves that sample with the time it was taken. Zero and anything over 99 are ignored. The raw samples are the stress array in the JSON. The averages, bands, and trend are not stored as their own fields.

These bands are labels in the app. They are not a confirmed QRing scale, and a 50 here is not a 50 on another device. 0–24 is Relaxed. 25–49 is Low. 50–74 is Moderate. 75–99 is High.

A sample is daytime when it falls outside every sleep window, from startedAt up to but not including endedAt. The Today circle shows the latest daytime sample from the phone's local day, with that band under it. The line under the circles is today's daytime average and peak. With no daytime sample it says "No daytime stress yet."

The Stress card, above Sleep, uses the same daytime samples. It draws them in time order, and shows the average, the peak, how long readings stayed at 75 or above, the share of samples in each band, and whether the last 3 hours are Rising, Falling, or Steady against the earlier part of today. That trend needs at least two samples on each side, and a gap of 5 points. High time counts each high sample as the gap until the next sample, capped at 60 minutes. The last sample of the day counts as 30 minutes.

A 7-day average of earlier daytime samples appears once two earlier days exist. It says how many days it used when that is under 7. A 30-day average appears once 8 earlier days exist. The ring keeps about a week, so the longer average only fills in as the phone keeps older samples.

Overnight is the average of samples inside the latest sleep window. It is labeled Overnight and is not given a stress band. A higher overnight reading is kept apart from the daytime number.

Body temperature

The card shows the latest finger reading in °C, with Fahrenheit and the stage beside it. The thermometer runs from 24°C to 40°C.

38°C (100.4°F) or higher is Fever. 35.0°C up to 38°C is Normal. Below 35°C (95°F) is Mild hypothermia, and that band runs down to 32°C. Below 32°C (89.6°F) is Moderate hypothermia. Below 28°C (82.4°F) is Severe hypothermia. Readings under 28°C used to be discarded. They are kept now so a severe reading can alert. A value still warming up, or anything under 20°C or over 43°C, is still ignored.

A Measure tap or a background sync notifies from that latest reading. The first time it crosses a line, the phone notifies. A worse line notifies again right away. The same stage waits 4 hours, then reminds. A normal reading clears the notice. Opening the app uses the saved reading the same way. A sync that does not include a temperature leaves the last notice alone.

The titles are "Body temperature is high", "Body temperature is low", "Body temperature is very low", and "Body temperature is dangerously low". Android posts these on a Body temperature channel. The stage is not its own JSON field. The upload still sends bodyTemperatureC.

Ring storage

The R09 does not keep a month. The useful store is about a week: heart rate in 5-minute slots (288 a day) once automatic checks are on, steps in 15-minute blocks (96 a day), stored blood oxygen and temperature in half-hour slots, and roughly a week of sleep nights. Days older than that come back empty. A full week of JSON is a few hundred kilobytes, not a large flash log. There is no published chip size worth planning around.

Duplicates

The phone already merges heart rate and steps by timestamp, so the saved reading does not grow duplicate minutes. The API still receives a whole snapshot whenever any sample is new, so the same minute can be posted again inside the next body.

A month-long cache is not worth it. The ring cannot resend anything older than about a week, so a month of keys does not make the filter safer. If Solloid appends rows, send only timestamps that were not in the last successful upload, and remember those timestamps for about 10 days. If Solloid replaces a row by timestamp, the full snapshot is fine and the phone does not need a second cache.

Two-day last reading

The Last reading Steps number is two days because, while the app is open, every log read asks the ring for two days. It is not a pile of readings that have not been sent yet.

While the phone is disconnected, the ring keeps its own history. Steps are stored in 15-minute blocks. Heart rate is stored about every 5 minutes. The ring does not send those blocks on its own. They stay on the ring until the phone connects and asks.

On a download, about every 4 hours or when Sync ring is tapped, the phone connects, sets the ring clock to UTC, and requests the stored logs. Steps are yesterday (day offset 1) and today (day offset 0). Heart rate is the log for those same two days. The ring replies with the blocks it has stored. The phone saves that reply as the last reading and disconnects. Days older than yesterday can still sit on the ring. This open-app read does not ask for them.

Last reading Steps adds every step block in that reply, so the number is yesterday plus today. The Today card does not. It keeps the blocks that fall on the phone's local calendar day. A successful upload does not drop yesterday from the next read. The next open-app sync asks for two days again.

A background wake, described above, asks for about 7 days instead. The phone also keeps earlier step and heart-rate samples by timestamp, so after a wake the Last reading total can cover more than the two days an open-app sync just requested. The ring still returns nothing older than about a week.

Sending to the Arduino IP

The last reading and the upload queue are different. After a read, the phone compares the new payload with the previous one. If something changed, the whole payload is added to a queue stored on the phone. About once an hour, and when Send waiting readings is tapped, the app POSTs each queued body as JSON to the API URL in Settings. Sync ring sends that queue right away. That URL is the Arduino, reached through the home network and port forwarding, for example http://203.0.113.10:8080/ingest. An optional key is sent as the X-Api-Key header. If the phone cannot reach that address, the queued readings stay on the phone and are tried again later. Sending or keeping that queue does not change what the ring returns on the next read.

Sleep upload

Sync posts one JSON body to the API URL (HTTP or the port-forwarded Arduino IP). Content-Type is application/json. A live sensor reading uses the same shape and sends "sleep": [].

Timestamps are UTC ISO-8601. The app sets the ring clock to UTC, and sleep start/end are minutes after that midnight. If start is later than end, sleep began the evening before the wake-up day.

The ring sends stages, not a score and not cycle numbers. The score shown in the app is the phone estimate described under Sleep score. Stage names are light, deep, rem, awake, and unknown (any code other than 2, 3, 4, or 5). minutesAsleep is light + deep + rem. minutesInBed is the start-to-end window. awakenings is how many awake stages were stored. A cycle is the phone grouping stages through the next REM block. complete is false when that stretch ended without REM.
